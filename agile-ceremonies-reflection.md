### Research & Learn

1. What are the four main Scrum ceremonies
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Standup
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective

2. What happens in each ceremony, and who participates?
a. Sprint Planning
The team decides what work to complete during the upcoming sprint and how they will approach it.
The following participate:
- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Developers
- QA testers

b. Daily Standup
The team has a short daily meeting to discuss progress, what they plan to do next, and anything blocking their work.
The following participate:
- Developers
- QA testers 
The Scrum Master and Product Owner may also attend, depending on how the team organises the meeting.

c. Sprint Review
The team presents the work completed during the sprint to stakeholders and gathers feedback.
The following participate:
- Scrum Team
- Stakeholders

d. Sprint Retrospective
The team looks back at the sprint to discuss what went well, what went wrong, and what they can improve in the next sprint.
The following participate:
- The Scrum Team: the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers, including testers.

3. What is QA’s role in each meeting?
a. Sprint Planning
I would review the features to be developed, ask questions about unclear requirements, identify possible risks, and plan how to test the features.

b. Daily Standup
I would share what I tested, any bugs I found, what I plan to test next, and any problems preventing me from making progress.

c. Sprint Review
I would explain what I tested, share important bugs discovered, and provide information about the quality of the features. I would also highlight any known issues that need further attention.

d. Sprint Retrospective
I would share testing challenges, suggest improvements, and discuss ways to prevent similar problems in future sprints.

4. How can testers use Sprint Planning to prepare for upcoming work?
- Understand what features will be developed.
- Ask questions about requirements and expected behaviour.
- Identify possible risks and challenging scenarios.
- Prepare test cases and decide what needs testing first.
- Identify devices, test data, or tools they will need.

5. Why is the Sprint Retrospective important for improving QA processes?
The Retrospective gives testers an opportunity to explain what made testing easier or more difficult and suggest practical improvements.



### Reflection

1. What should QA do before, during, and after a Sprint Planning meeting?
Before the meeting:
- Review the features and requirements planned for the sprint.
- Think about what needs to be tested.
- Prepare questions about anything that is unclear.
- Identify possible risks and testing needs.

During the meeting:
- Ask questions to understand how each feature should work.
- Identify possible problems and unusual situations to test.
- Discuss the time and resources needed for testing.
- Make sure testing is considered when planning the work.

After the meeting:
- Prepare test cases or checklists.
- Get the necessary test data, devices, and tools ready.
- Organise testing tasks according to priority.
- Stay in touch with developers so testing can begin as soon as features are ready.

While interning at Assurdly, I occasionally participated in daily stand-up meetings where I shared what I had tested the previous day, what I planned to test that day, and any blockers I had encountered. Our stand-up meetings were held three times a week and were attended by the testers, Product Manager, and Project Manager.

2. If a Daily Standup is running long, what’s the best way to keep QA updates concise?
I would keep my update short and focus on three things: 
- what I tested
- what I will test next
- blockers

3. How can QA drive continuous improvement during Sprint Retrospectives?
QA can help the team improve by sharing testing challenges and suggesting practical ways to prevent them from happening again.

For example:
- If features arrive late, suggest involving QA earlier.
- If requirements are unclear, suggest reviewing acceptance criteria before development starts.
- If the same bugs keep returning, suggest adding regression tests.
